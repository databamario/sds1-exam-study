Read L4, L5 and L6: twenty questions missing a page, two saying things the slides do not

Twenty-two questions. Two were wrong in substance and twenty had a second page
they should have cited but did not. That is a much higher rate than the earlier
batches found, and the reason is visible in the table: the lectures split single
ideas across consecutive slides, so the question sits on the seam.

L4-MC-009 is the clearest case. "What is a confidence interval", "what happens at
99%", and "how often does it cover the truth" are p.32, p.33 and p.34 respectively,
and the question asks all three. Under the old format two of them had to go. It
now cites all three and the two other CI questions sit on their own slides.

The rest follow the same shape. p.16 defines disjoint and uses the same red/black
card that L4-MC-002 asks about, while the p.17 it cited only covers complementary.
p.36 is where "with replacement draws are independent" is printed, and L4-MC-013 was
citing p.37, which is entirely about without replacement. p.35 defines an estimator
as a function of the data and a random variable, which is the whole keyed content
of L5-MC-008, while p.37 only shows the sample mean is unbiased. p.22 turned out to
be a figure reference whose text layer carries no formula at all, so L4-WA-005 now
cites p.21, where the rule is actually worked.

Two questions were wrong in the way that is harder to see. L5-MC-015 keyed a claim
about symmetry and the mean and median coinciding, which p.21 never mentions; its
options now say what p.21's table says. Its explanation also claimed the mode is
"still perfectly well defined for a skewed distribution", which p.21 does not say
either. And L4-WA-004 asked about drawing two cards while its rubric demanded the
bag's 3/10 versus 2/9, so it asked about the bag, which is what the answer and the
cited page actually use.
